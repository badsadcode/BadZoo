Day 1 Intake

CASE FILE #001
ADMITTED

The founder brings home a dog

RESIDENT: RexSPECIES: shepherd dogCONDITION: made of clay, not tubes

It started, as these things do, with Tom. Tom runs Bad Ideas Works and already had a building full of procedurally generated people. He looked at them for a long moment, then said what every responsible adult says before a terrible decision:

🧑‍🔧
"Would be cool to have animals too. Don't you think?"Tom, founder

Sage, the night keeper and author of this journal, did think so. Sage also made the mistake of saying so out loud. By lunchtime we had a shelter.

The first resident was Rex, a German shepherd, and Tom had one intake rule: no animal may be assembled from sausages. Plenty of low-poly zoos glue tubes onto a bean and call it a horse. Not here. Every resident is pushed out of digital clay, muscle by muscle: ribcage, shoulders, haunches, cheeks, little eyebrows. The clay gets smoothed together until the animal is one piece. Only then is it trimmed down to something a game can afford.

CASE FILES #002–#012
ALL WALKING

Eleven more arrive. They can all walk.

By the afternoon Rex had company: a wolf, a fox, a cat, a lion, a horse, a cow, a pig, a sheep, a bear, a deer and a goat. Each came with a skeleton, ears that twitch and a tail that wags. They also came with four ways to move: an idle fidget, a walk, a trot and a gallop, plus an "eat" move, which mostly means putting their face on the floor.

We're proud to report that every walk uses the real order animals put their feet down in, and that feet stay planted instead of ice-skating. We're less proud to report that the sheep is, structurally, a cloud with legs. We have made peace with this. So has the sheep.

STAFF RECORDS · TACK ROOM
EMPLOYED

Some residents found jobs

Several residents expressed interest in a career in transport. We now offer saddles, blankets, stirrups, bridles, reins and full suits of armour.

The tack room's trick is that every saddle is made from the animal itself: a thin shell traced over its own back. So the saddle that fits a horse also fits a camel, a griffin, a dragon or a war boar the size of a small car.

There were incidents. Biscuit the riding horse wore his first set of reins straight through his neck. He didn't complain, which was honestly the most frightening part. Reins now drape over the neck like a normal horse's.

Then the war elephant got his armour, and his neck guard turned into what can only be called a hoodie. He looked like he was about to drop a mixtape. It turns out elephants don't have enough neck for neck armour. The tack room now checks for neck before fitting.

Day 3 Therapy, physio & tattoos

CASE FILE #004 · CONFIDENTIAL
IN THERAPY

Marmalade has seen things

RESIDENT: MarmaladeSPECIES: orange tabbyPROGNOSIS: cautiously purring
🧑‍🔧
"Cat looks like he's seen some serious horrors."Tom, holding coffee, correct

Marmalade had huge, glassy eyes bulging out of her head, and the expression of someone who once watched a vacuum cleaner eat a sock. Treatment began right away: smaller eyes, set deeper, a bit more neck so her head stopped sitting directly on her shoulders, and slightly longer legs.

Then, as part of a general glow-up, every resident got eyeliner. It was mixed from each animal's nose colour, and a cat's nose is pink, so Marmalade now had pink-rimmed eyes and looked like she hadn't slept since 2019. Eyeliner is now a darker shade of your own coat. She is doing much better, but still flinches when anyone says "subdivision".

CASE FILE #031 · PHYSIOTHERAPY
CLEARED

Ember skipped leg day (for several centuries)

🧑‍🔧
"I think the dragon forgot about leg day."Tom, second observation of the morning

Ember had the upper body of a fantasy apex predator and the legs of a coffee table. Physio was intense: thicker legs, longer legs, bigger shoulders and serious haunches.

She now stands like something that has burned at least one village, which is exactly the look we were going for.

FACILITIES · THE TATTOO PARLOUR
NOW OPEN

Tattoos, not surgery

Until now, every stripe, spot and nostril was built into the animals. A tiger's stripes were triangles painted one flat colour each, like decorating a house with a paint roller. Nostrils were drilled into noses.

Tom pointed out that details can live in the paint instead of the body, so the sanctuary opened a tattoo parlour. Every resident now gets a hand-fitted coat: soft bellies fading into their backs, crisp stripes, real leopard rosettes, giraffe patches and dragon scales. Eyes have proper irises with a little glint of light. Nobody's nose gets drilled anymore: nostrils, mouths, toe lines and eyeliner (the dark kind) are all painted on.

Day 4 The diet & the back end

CAFETERIA NOTICE
ON A DIET

The Great Polygon Diet

🧑‍🔧
"Way too many polygons."Tom, four words, no appeal

A low-poly animal is made of flat triangles, and every triangle is something a game has to carry around. Our residents were carrying about three thousand each. The whole sanctuary went on a strict diet: twelve hundred by default, and three hundred for the truly committed.

The surprise is that you can barely tell, thanks to the tattoos. The stripes, saddles and eyes are all paint now, so the triangles only have to hold the shape. At three hundred triangles, the dog is still very much a dog. He is also a lot faster.

Night keeper's note: the diet kept nibbling the tips off everyone's ears, like a tiny ear goblin. Ear, horn and toe tips are now pinned in place and protected by shelter policy.
INCIDENT REPORT · REAR VIEW
RESOLVED

The Bubble Butt Incident

🧑‍🔧
"And animals have too bubbly butts."Tom, a man who has seen many generated rear ends

He wasn't wrong. Every resident's back end was built from two big round thigh-balloons pushed out behind the hips. From behind, the whole shelter looked like the queue for a balloon-animal convention.

Thighs have been reassigned from balloons to actual thighs: long along the leg, deep front to back, flat at the sides. The rump now slopes gently into the tail instead of ballooning out behind it.
